What Makes a Good Research Note

The AMJ “Publishing in AMJ” series (2011–2012) — same principles at research note scale:

Section AMJ principle Applied here
Introduction “Set the hook” — the gap, not the topic What is unknown and why it matters
Theory Hypotheses must be derived, not asserted Theory → mechanism → prediction → H
Data Document every sample choice Filters, N, date range, variable table
Results Interpret, don’t just report Every coefficient: one plain-language sentence
Discussion “Broaden the return” — back to theory What does your result mean for the theories you cited?

AMJ Part 3: “The introduction should tell readers what you studied, why it is interesting, what you did, and what you found — in that order.”

AMJ Part 4: “A hypothesis is not a prediction. It is a theoretically derived argument.”

Good vs Weak — At a Glance

Element Good Weak
RQ One focused falsifiable question Multiple vague questions
Theory 2–3 theories → mechanism → H Theory listed, not used to derive H
H derivation Explicit logic chain in text H stated without justification
Data Source, N, filters documented “Data from WRDS” — no detail
Method FE justified, SEs cited OLS only, no discussion of bias
Results Each coefficient interpreted Table only — “as shown in Table 2”
Discussion Links result back to cited theory “Results were significant”
Limitations Two specific, honest limitations None, or vague “more research needed”

The examiner’s core question: could someone reproduce your result, and understand what it means, by reading this note alone?

Quarto — File Extension & YAML Header

Use .qmd not .md — Quarto requires the .qmd extension for documents with executable Python chunks. Name your file research_note.qmd.

---
title: "Your Title"
subtitle: "A Panel Data Analysis"
author: |
  Your Name | ExInt II | WU Vienna | SS 2026
  https://github.com/[your-username]/[your-repo]
date: "June 2026"
bibliography: references/library.bib
csl: references/apa.csl
format:
  pdf:
    toc: false
    number-sections: true
    geometry: margin=2.5cm
    fontsize: 11pt
    linestretch: 1.3
execute:
  eval: true      # run Python chunks → tables and numbers update automatically
  echo: false     # hide code from PDF
  warning: false
  message: false
---

GitHub URL in author: block → appears on title page under your name.

Taskfile — Correct Setup

render has no deps — standalone so task render doesn’t trigger the whole pipeline.
all runs the full chain and then renders:

  render:
    desc: "Render research_note.qmd to PDF via Quarto"
    cmds:
      - quarto render research_note.qmd

  all:
    desc: "Full pipeline — analysis + PDF"
    deps: [regression]
    cmds:
      - quarto render research_note.qmd

So: task render → PDF only. task all → pull → clean → descriptives → regression → PDF.

Fix the apa.csl error — download and commit once:

Invoke-WebRequest `
  -Uri "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/citation-style-language/styles/master/apa.csl" `
  -OutFile "references\apa.csl"
git add references/apa.csl && git commit -m "add apa.csl" && git push

Quarto — Markdown Syntax

# Section heading (auto-numbered: "1 Introduction")
## Subsection ("1.1 Background")

Normal paragraph. **Bold.** *Italic.*

Inline citation:      [@cohenAbsorptiveCapacityNew1990]
Multiple citations:   [@cohenAbsorptiveCapacityNew1990; @grilichesIssuesAssessingContribution1979]
Author as subject:    @petersenEstimatingStandardErrors2009 show that...

Hypothesis block quote:
> **H1:** [Your hypothesis in one sentence.]
> *(Test: β(variable) [sign] and significant)*

Inline math:    $\beta_1 < 0$
Display math:
$$\text{RoA}_{it} = \beta_1 X_{it} + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{it}$$

Citation key must match library.bib exactly — check by opening the file and looking at the @article{key, line. Zotero generates keys like cohenAbsorptiveCapacityNew1990.

Writing the Introduction — AMJ “Setting the Hook”

AMJ Part 3: two failure modes — the topic introduction (describes a field, never states a gap) and the methods introduction (jumps to what you did before establishing why it matters).

Four-element structure:

[Paragraph 1] — What is known and why it matters.
Cite 2-3 papers establishing the field.

[Paragraph 2] — What is NOT known (the gap).
This is where your RQ lives.

[Paragraph 3] — What this note does and what it finds.
One sentence each: sample, method, main result.

> **Research question:** [One sentence, ending with a question mark.]

AMJ Part 3: “Effective introductions tell readers something they do not already know. Describing ‘a large literature on X’ is not a gap — it is a topic. The gap is what that literature has not yet answered.”

Writing the Theory Section — AMJ “Grounding Hypotheses”

AMJ Part 4: a hypothesis without a theoretical mechanism is a guess.

Structure for each hypothesis:

[Paragraph 1] — Theory: what does [theory] claim? Cite 2 papers.

[Paragraph 2] — Mechanism: how does this apply in your context?
Why does [theory] predict [direction] for your specific sample?

[Paragraph 3] — Boundary condition (if any): why SMEs, why Europe?

> **H1:** [Directional claim in one sentence.]
> *(Test: expected sign of coefficient)*

AMJ Part 4: “The best hypotheses surprise you a little. If your H is obvious from the first sentence of the paper, you have not built an argument — you have stated a fact.”

Writing Results — Interpret, Don’t Just Report

AMJ Part 6: “Results sections that simply recite statistics add nothing. Readers can read a table. Tell them what the number means.”

Three required elements per hypothesis:

**H1.** [Variable] is [sign] and [significant/not significant]
(β = `{python} b_x`, p < 0.01, Model 2).
**H1 is [supported / not supported].**
[One plain-language sentence: what does this coefficient mean?]

**OLS vs FE.** The OLS estimate (`{python} b_ols`) differs from
the FE estimate (`{python} b_fe`) by `{python} pct`%, confirming
omitted variable bias [@hausmanSpecificationTestsEconometrics1978].

Use inline {python} expressions to pull coefficients directly — numbers match the table automatically.

Writing Discussion & Conclusion — AMJ “The Return”

AMJ Part 7: the discussion is the “return trip” from data back to theory.

Discussion — one paragraph:

  • Restate main finding in one sentence
  • Link back to the specific theory you cited — what does this tell us about that theory?
  • Note one unexpected or nuanced pattern
  • One real-world implication

Conclusion — one paragraph:

  • Answer the RQ directly: “This note finds that…”
  • Two specific limitations — not “more research is needed”
  • One concrete future research direction

AMJ Part 7: “The discussion is your opportunity to show that you understand what your findings mean — not just that they are statistically significant. Significance is the floor, not the ceiling.”
